## Question and claim limits

At an offseason cutoff, does one learned age/reliability multiplier order MLB assets by their next three seasons of reference production better than a flat multiplier and the current hand-set age/reliability multiplier, when all three share exactly the same year-1 Marcel forecast?

The estimand is ordering of origin-eligible MLB assets by the charter's weighted H3, in its fixed 5x5 reference units above zero production. Higher is better. A result can support only the declared retrospective ranking comparison, subject to admission and exposure limits. It cannot establish dollars, market price, calibration of display scores, redraft improvement, prospect performance, lineup utility, package fairness, replacement-aware trade value, or qualification of other league presets.

Only the multi-year age/reliability multiplier is learned. The 1.0/0.72/0.48 year weights express a dynasty preference and define the target; they are fixed, not learnable facts. Sharing F1 prevents a gain from merely using more history than the comparators. Any gap comes only from the multipliers, so gaps may be small and the 0.02 bar may be hard to clear. Do not lower it. A fail is a legitimate result: the hand-set curves stay, with no retune. The prospective prediction freeze may still run as shadow under separate authority.

The complete production incumbent cannot be reconstructed historically without origin-available archived Steamer forecasts. The hand-set comparator reconstructs the whole preseason age and playing-time chain: AgeCurve and VolumeMultiplier on year-1 value, then the horizon's age and reliability factors, on production's centered basis around the pool minimum. It omits scaling, role anchor, track-record floors, the 0.05 reliability blend, and the in-season stability blend, which is inactive in preseason. A pass cannot establish superiority over the full incumbent. Transfer additionally assumes Marcel versus Steamer as year-1 forecast; the origin universe versus the live eligible Steamer pool for centering and the baseline; reference category SDs versus live-league SDs; and GS/G routing versus production position eligibility. Predictions rank in target units, while production's cross-family order also depends on centering and the role anchor, which this study does not test. Only prospective scoring can test these transfer assumptions. Shipping is a separate owner decision; no result automatically changes the public board.

### Exactly one candidate

At every origin, construct the same F1, age, reliability, and baseline for all three orderings using only seasons through y:

- F1 is the routed family's U for y+1 from build_marcel_projections or build_pitcher_projections with default parameters and target_season=y+1, translated into origin-y reference units. - Let age = age_for(dob, y+1), completed years on April 1, and a = clamp(age, 22, 37) - 29. The clamp matches the source curve range; the review fixes 29 as PEAK_AGE. In-season production prefers feed metadata age; no historical feed was archived, so that path is not reconstructable.
- rho = max(0.55, min(1, round(100 * vol / (vol + reg), 2) / 100)), with rho=0.55 when vol <= 0. vol is Marcel's projected routed-role PA or IP; reg is 150 for hitter, 20 for RP, 45 for SP. Round the percentage to two decimals before dividing by 100. This is the production playing-time reliability formula and future floor.
- C_fam is production's centering in reference units. For each family (hitter; SP and RP together) it is the sum over that family's categories of the mean forecast category impact, over the frozen universe's rows of that family, divided by that category's reference SD. V = F1 - C_fam is production's centered year-1 value. Centering does not cancel against a minimum pooled across families whose C_fam differ, so V rather than F1 enters the multiplier.
- B is min(V) over the frozen origin-y universe, all families pooled, mirroring horizon_baseline in mlb/dynasty.py. Bind this universe before labels; do not compute C_fam or B by gate or matched subset. Compute them over universe rows that have an F1. A row without one counts against coverage and does not block them.

Every ordering uses H3_hat = C_fam + B + (V - B) * m, which ranks in target units, so m = 1 gives F1. The one candidate uses the routed role's m = max(0, b0 + b1*a + b2*a^2 + b3*rho). V - B is nonnegative, so the multiplier cannot reverse its sign. There are four coefficients per role, 12 total, with no post-fit calibration or role-percentile mapping.

Fit closed-form ridge separately for hitter, SP, and RP. Each training origin z uses its own C_fam,z, V_z and B_z. The response is realized H3 - C_fam,z - B_z. In fixed order the four features are (V_z-B_z), (V_z-B_z)*a, (V_z-B_z)*a^2, (V_z-B_z)*rho. Divide each feature by its training-fold population SD, computed on eligible training rows for that role; do not center features and do not fit an additive intercept. Use scale 1 for a zero-SD feature, retaining its uncentered values. Apply scales unchanged to test rows and divide fitted coefficients by those scales to obtain b0 through b3 in the displayed multiplier. Clamp m at prediction time as specified.

Use equal weight per asset-origin training row. Minimize the sum of squared residuals for that response plus 10.0 times the sum of squares of all four standardized-feature coefficients, using a closed-form linear solve. Do not divide the residual sum by training n and thereby change the penalty convention. No training rows for a required role, nonfinite inputs, or an unsolved fit block that fold; do not switch estimators.

Lambda is fixed at 10.0, matching the project's RIDGE_LAMBDA. This is reuse of a project constant, not empirical selection for this task. Zero searches, feature selection, early stopping, extra feature expansions, alternate candidates, or named-player adjustments. Historical U and historical volume are not additional ridge features.
